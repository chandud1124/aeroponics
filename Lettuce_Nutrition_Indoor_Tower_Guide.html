What to do: Soak rockwool cubes in plain water adjusted to pH 5.5 for 1 hour. No nutrients at all. Rockwool ships at pH 7–8 — soaking brings it to correct range for seeds. Drain excess water — cubes should be moist, not dripping.
Why pH 5.5 for rockwool soak: Seeds germinate best between pH 5.5–6.0. High pH in rockwool (7–8) causes poor germination. One hour soak fixes this permanently.

Final flush option (Day 33–35): 3 days before harvest, some growers replace nutrient solution with clean pH 5.8 water only. This reduces salt taste in leaves and improves flavour. Completely optional — leaves still taste fine without flushing.
Why reduce PPM before harvest?
Reducing EC from 1.2 to 0.8–1.0 in the final week forces the plant to use stored nutrients from existing leaves. This concentrates flavour compounds, produces slightly crisper texture, and reduces bitterness. High EC lettuce at harvest can taste slightly bitter or metallic.

Deficiency signs — what to look for and exact fix

Each deficiency has a characteristic visual pattern. Identify by WHERE on the plant the symptom appears.

Diagnosis rule: Old leaves (bottom) affected = mobile nutrient deficiency (N, P, K, Mg). New leaves (top) affected = immobile nutrient deficiency (Ca, Fe, Mn, B). pH wrong first = correct pH before adding any nutrients.
Visual deficiency identification
Nitrogen (N) deficiency — most common in lettuce Very Common

What you see: Uniform yellowing of OLDER (lower) leaves first. Starts at leaf tips and spreads inward. Plant looks pale overall. Growth slows dramatically. New top leaves stay green longer.

Why it happens: EC too low (below 0.8 mS/cm), pH too high (above 6.5 locks N uptake), or reservoir nutrient solution is exhausted and needs replacement.

Immediate fixIncrease Part A dose by 20%. Check pH (must be below 6.5).
Increase EC to1.0–1.2 mS/cm (700–840 PPM)
pH must be5.8–6.2 for N absorption
Recovery time4–7 days after correction
PreventionCheck EC daily. Never let EC drop below 0.7 mS/cm.
Calcium (Ca) deficiency — tip burn — very common indoors Very Common Indoor

What you see: Brown, dry, crispy edges on the NEWEST (inner) leaves. Tips and margins look burned. Outer leaves fine, inner leaves show the damage first. Sometimes entire leaf edge turns brown and papery.

Why it happens: Calcium is immobile — plant cannot move it from old to new cells. New growth cells have weak walls and collapse. Caused by: too high EC, poor airflow (Ca moves by transpiration), high humidity, or actual Ca deficiency in solution.

Immediate fixAdd 20g/100L extra Cal-Nitrate. Turn on fan near plants.
Ca target in solution100–150 ppm Ca²⁺
EC limit for lettuceNever above 1.4 mS/cm — causes tip burn
AirflowFan blowing at 0.3–0.5 m/s across leaf canopy
HumidityKeep below 70% — high humidity stops Ca movement
Iron (Fe) deficiency — interveinal chlorosis on new leaves Common

What you see: NEW (young) leaves turn yellow, but the VEINS remain distinctly green. The green vein pattern on a yellow background is a clear iron deficiency signature. Old leaves look fine initially.

Why it happens: Almost always pH too high (above 6.5). Iron becomes insoluble above pH 6.5 even if plenty is in solution. Real iron deficiency only when pH is correct.

First checkpH — must be 5.5–6.2. Correct pH before adding iron.
If pH correctAdd 5g/100L chelated iron (Fe-EDTA)
Use chelated formEDTA-Fe or DTPA-Fe stays available at pH up to 6.5
Recovery time5–10 days — new growth will appear green
Old yellow leavesWill not recover — trim them off
Magnesium (Mg) deficiency — interveinal chlorosis on OLD leaves Moderate

What you see: OLDER (lower) leaves turn yellow between the veins. Veins stay green on old leaves — opposite of iron deficiency which affects new leaves. Leaves may also develop reddish or purplish tinge in severe cases.

Why it happens: Magnesium is mobile — plant pulls it from old leaves to feed new growth. Most common when using A+B nutrients without supplemental Epsom salt, or when reservoir solution is old and Mg depleted.

Immediate fixAdd 50g/100L Epsom salt (MgSO₄) to reservoir
Foliar spray3g Epsom salt per litre — spray directly on leaves for fast recovery
Mg target in solution40–60 ppm Mg²⁺
PreventionAlways add Epsom salt at every reservoir refill
Recovery time3–5 days with foliar spray, 7 days via root uptake
Potassium (K) deficiency — brown leaf margins starting from older leaves Less Common

What you see: Leaf edges turn yellow then brown starting at the tips and margins of OLDER leaves. Unlike Ca tip burn (crispy and inner leaves), K deficiency creates a scorched margin pattern around the full leaf perimeter. Stems become weak and floppy.

Why it happens: Not enough potassium in Part B solution, or reservoir is old and K depleted.

FixIncrease Part B dose by 20%. Do full reservoir change.
K target200–250 ppm K⁺
Use 8:15:36 formulaHigh K ratio specifically for leafy greens
Don't confuse withCa tip burn (Ca = inner new leaves; K = outer old leaves)
Root rot (Pythium) — brown/slimy roots, wilting despite misting Emergency

What you see: Plant wilts even though misting is running. Pull the net pot — roots are brown, slimy, and smell bad. Healthy roots are WHITE and firm. Brown roots = early stage. Black slimy roots = advanced stage.

Causes: Water temperature above 24°C, insufficient dissolved oxygen in reservoir, Pythium spores in contaminated water or growing media, or waterlogged net pots from wrong hole angle.

Immediate actionAdd 5 ml/L H₂O₂ 3% to reservoir. Cool water below 22°C.
Remove affected plantsTrim brown roots. Treat with dilute H₂O₂. Replant.
Reduce mistingOverwatering = root rot. Try 3 min ON / 8 min OFF.
Add Trichoderma1g/10L at next reservoir change — beneficial fungi protection
Water temp targetKeep below 22°C. Above 26°C = Pythium grows rapidly.

Weekly actions (every 7 days)
Every 7 days — chemical actions
Partial reservoir changeRemove 30% of solution, replace with fresh nutrient mix
H₂O₂ treatmentAdd 3ml/L hydrogen peroxide to reservoir — kills bacteria
Nozzle soakRemove nozzles, soak 30 min in 1g/L citric acid, rinse with water
pH/EC sensor calibrationUse buffer solutions to verify meter accuracy
Root inspectionPull one net pot — check roots are white and healthy
Every 14–21 days — full reset
Full reservoir drainEmpty completely. Wash with 3% H₂O₂. Rinse twice with clean water.
Mix fresh nutrient solutionFollow stage-appropriate dosage from Stage chart above

PPM & pH adjustment cheat sheet
If PPM / EC is wrong
ReadingProblemExact fix
Below 350 PPM (EC <0.5)StarvationAdd 100ml Part A + 100ml Part B per 100L. Recheck EC.
350–560 PPM (EC 0.5–0.8)Slightly lowAdd 50ml A + 50ml B per 100L. Good for seedlings only.
560–840 PPM (EC 0.8–1.2)Perfect rangeNo action — maintain this range
840–1000 PPM (EC 1.2–1.4)Slightly highAdd 10L plain pH-adjusted water. Dilutes without wasting nutrients.
Above 1000 PPM (EC >1.4)Tip burn riskDrain 20% of reservoir. Replace with plain pH water. Do NOT add more nutrients until EC drops.
If pH is wrong
ReadingEffect on lettuceFix
Below 5.0Root acid burn, heavy metal toxicityAdd pH Up 0.5ml. Wait 5 min. Repeat if needed.
5.0–5.5Slightly acidic — OK for short termSmall dose pH Up. Monitor closely.
5.5–6.0PERFECT for lettuceNo action — this is ideal
6.0–6.5Acceptable — slight iron reductionSmall dose pH Down if drifting toward 6.5
Above 6.5Iron/Mn lockout — new yellow leavesAdd pH Down 1ml. Wait 5 min. Repeat until 6.0. Check Fe-EDTA dose.
Above 7.0Multiple nutrient lockouts — plant failureEmergency: add pH Down 2ml, wait 10 min, recheck. Do partial reservoir change if persistent.
